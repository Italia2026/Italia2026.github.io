Important Italian Words & Phrases

The essentials, with rough pronunciation.

Greetings & courtesy
  • Buongiorno bwon-JOR-noGood morning / hello
  • Buonasera bwo-na-SEH-raGood evening
  • Ciao chowHi / bye (informal)
  • Arrivederci ar-ree-veh-DER-cheeGoodbye
  • Per favore per fa-VOH-rehPlease
  • Grazie GRAH-tsyehThank you
  • Prego PREH-goYou're welcome / go ahead
  • Scusi SKOO-zeeExcuse me (formal)
  • Mi dispiace mee dee-SPYAH-chehI'm sorry
Getting by
  • Sì / No see / noYes / No
  • Non capisco non ka-PEES-koI don't understand
  • Parla inglese? PAR-la een-GLEH-zehDo you speak English?
  • Non parlo italiano non PAR-lo ee-ta-LYAH-noI don't speak Italian
  • Come si dice…? KOH-meh see DEE-chehHow do you say…?
  • Va bene va BEH-nehOkay / that's fine
Getting around & directions
  • Dov'è il bagno? do-VEH eel BAH-nyoWhere's the bathroom?
  • La stazione la sta-TSYOH-nehThe train station
  • Un biglietto per… oon bee-LYET-to perA ticket to…
  • A che ora parte? a keh OH-ra PAR-tehWhat time does it leave?
  • Quanto dista? KWAN-to DEE-staHow far is it?
  • A sinistra / A destra a see-NEES-tra / a DES-traLeft / Right
  • Sempre dritto SEM-preh DREET-toStraight ahead
Numbers
  • uno · due · tre OO-no · DOO-eh · treh1 · 2 · 3
  • quattro · cinque · sei KWAT-tro · CHEEN-kweh · say4 · 5 · 6
  • sette · otto · nove · dieci SET-teh · OT-to · NOH-veh · DYEH-chee7 · 8 · 9 · 10
  • venti · cento VEN-tee · CHEN-to20 · 100
Shopping & money
  • Quanto costa? KWAN-to KOS-taHow much is it?
  • Troppo caro TROP-po KAH-roToo expensive
  • Solo sto guardando SO-lo sto gwar-DAN-doJust looking
  • Accettate carte? ach-chet-TAH-teh KAR-tehDo you take cards?
Emergencies
  • Aiuto! ah-YOO-toHelp!
  • Chiami la polizia KYAH-mee la po-lee-TSEE-aCall the police
  • Ho bisogno di un medico o bee-ZOH-nyo dee oon MEH-dee-koI need a doctor
  • Chiami un'ambulanza KYAH-mee oo-nam-boo-LAN-tsaCall an ambulance
  • Mi sono perso / persa mee SO-no PER-so / PER-saI'm lost (m/f)
Nice to meet you
  • Come stai? KOH-meh styHow are you?
  • Bene, grazie BEH-neh GRAH-tsyehWell, thanks
  • Mi chiamo… mee KYAH-moMy name is…
  • Piacere pya-CHEH-rehNice to meet you

Two habits that go a long way

Use scusi (formal) rather than scusa with strangers, and open with buongiorno when you walk into a shop or café — in Italy it's basic manners, not optional.
